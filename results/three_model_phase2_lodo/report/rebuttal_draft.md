We tested whether one alignment adapter transfers to a dataset it was never fitted on. Leaving one dataset out at a time, for each of the 14 datasets we fitted a single label-free affine adapter on the remaining datasets (13 sources, or 12 when an Electricity dataset is the target, because the hourly and 15-minute Electricity datasets share meters) with the same objective, optimizer and checkpoint selection as the dataset-specific adapters, and evaluated it once on the held-out dataset's test windows with no target-specific fitting or selection. Truncating at L4, L6, L9 (measured end-to-end speedups 2.43x / 1.78x / 1.28x at batch size 1), the median test MASE change relative to the full model was +37.0% / +20.1% / +17.1% for hard truncation, +12.7% / +9.0% / +4.0% for the pooled adapter and +3.1% / +2.3% / +2.2% for the dataset-specific adapter. The pooled adapter beat hard truncation on 13 of 14 datasets at L4 (13 with a paired 95% CI below 1 for the MASE ratio); 13 of 14 datasets at L6 (13 with a paired 95% CI below 1 for the MASE ratio); 13 of 14 datasets at L9 (13 with a paired 95% CI below 1 for the MASE ratio). On the datasets documented as held out from Chronos-2 pretraining (Loop Seattle, M5, SZ Taxi, Traffic), the pooled adapter's median change was +15.5% / +15.3% / +1.8% (hard truncation +8.4% / +10.6% / +6.3%). The truncation depth was fixed in advance; selecting a depth for an unseen dataset without calibration data is a separate question that this experiment does not address.
