**Dataset-specific calibration and zero-shot acceleration.** To test whether the remaining gap between zero-shot and dataset-specific alignment reflects the small number of source datasets, we fitted the same label-free adapter to Chronos-2 truncated after block 6 of 12 (measured end-to-end speedup 1.78x at batch size 1) on N = 1, 4, 8 and 13 source datasets, never using the target dataset for fitting or model selection, for four target datasets chosen in advance from metadata alone: Loop Seattle (5-minute), London Smart Meters (30-minute), BOOM (hourly) and M5 (daily), which cover all three of our pretraining-provenance groups. For N below 13 we used three nested random orders of the source datasets per target. With all 13 other datasets as sources, the test MASE of the truncated model was +12.8% relative to the full model (mean over 4 targets), against +3.2% for the dataset-specific adapter and +19.8% for truncation without an adapter (per target, pooled vs dataset-specific: Loop Seattle +26.2% vs -3.6%; London Smart Meters +9.6% vs +4.6%; BOOM +11.2% vs +9.3%; M5 +4.3% vs +2.3%). The 13-source adapter was statistically indistinguishable from, or better than, the dataset-specific adapter for 0 of 4 targets and worse for 4 (paired 95% bootstrap intervals over test series). Across source counts, the mean degradation decreased from N = 1 to N = 13 (+19.7%, +12.7%, +13.2%, +12.8% at N = 1, 4, 8, 13); going from one source to 13 reduced it with a paired interval excluding zero in 8 of the 12 target and order combinations and increased it in 2, and 3 of the 12 target and order combinations never got worse as sources were added. These results describe our 14-dataset collection only: each added source also adds training windows (1,394 per dataset), the two Electricity datasets share meters (so N = 13 spans 12 dataset families), and we do not extrapolate beyond 13 sources. In particular, they do not show that a larger and more diverse source collection would make zero-shot alignment match dataset-specific alignment.
