We ran this experiment for Chronos-2 on all 14 datasets. At a fixed number of retained blocks (4, 6 or 9) we compared the prefix used in the paper with three predefined masks fixed before evaluation: uniform spacing, early plus late blocks, and a middle contiguous window as a control. Skipped blocks are removed from the pretrained backbone (retained blocks keep their order and weights), and every configuration receives the same dataset-specific label-free adapter, objective and selection rule as in the paper. Test MASE relative to the full model, median over datasets: with 4 of 12 blocks the prefix degrades MASE by a median +3.1%, uniform +8.9% (better than prefix on 0 and worse on 10 of 14 datasets, paired CI), early + late +10.1% (better than prefix on 0 and worse on 11 of 14 datasets, paired CI), middle contiguous +7.2% (better than prefix on 0 and worse on 12 of 14 datasets, paired CI); with 6 of 12 blocks the prefix degrades MASE by a median +2.3%, uniform +4.2% (better than prefix on 4 and worse on 4 of 14 datasets, paired CI), early + late +7.6% (better than prefix on 1 and worse on 7 of 14 datasets, paired CI), middle contiguous +7.6% (better than prefix on 1 and worse on 12 of 14 datasets, paired CI); with 9 of 12 blocks the prefix degrades MASE by a median +2.2%, uniform +0.9% (better than prefix on 7 and worse on 1 of 14 datasets, paired CI), early + late +0.9% (better than prefix on 6 and worse on 1 of 14 datasets, paired CI), middle contiguous +3.4% (better than prefix on 1 and worse on 6 of 14 datasets, paired CI). Removing a single block without any adapter is most damaging for L12 (median +18.5%) and least for L7 (median -0.0%). Latency was measured, not assumed; end-to-end speedups at batch size 1 (prefix, uniform, early plus late, middle) were k=4: 2.36x, 2.35x, 2.37x, 2.24x; k=6: 1.72x, 1.76x, 1.75x, 1.81x; k=9: 1.27x, 1.28x, 1.25x, 1.24x. Because skipping feeds retained blocks inputs they never saw in pretraining, a weaker non-contiguous mask does not imply that the skipped blocks are unimportant.
